  - With [`AllyReveal=yes`](/keys/allyreveal/), a house that allies with the local player's house reveals to that player the ground its objects can see. [Map visibility](/systems/map-visibility/) owns which objects can look.

## When a direction is refused

A house refuses to ally with the other house while **any of** these holds:

- it already counts the other house an ally;
- it has been defeated;
- the other house is an observer;
- it has only one opponent left. Opponents are the houses it is not allied with, counting only houses that are neither defeated nor of a [passive country](/keys/multiplaypassive/).

The last rule refuses a house whose only opponent is the house it is allying with. If both houses are in that position, as when they are the only two houses left that are neither defeated nor of a passive country, nothing changes. If only one of them is, the alliance is one-sided.

## When a human player's house is involved

With [`Paranoid=yes`](/keys/paranoid/), which the stock rules set, a human player's house that allies with a house of a non-passive country changes the computer houses' alliances. Every computer house still in the game breaks any alliance with each human house still in the game and gains anger toward it. It also tries to ally with each other computer house. A computer house that was allied with a human house when the scenario was set up is left out while that human house is still in the game, and the other computer houses do not ally with it. In a campaign, the player's house counts as a human player's house.

This realignment changes the outcome of an alliance between a human house and a computer house:

- If the trigger's house is the human house, the computer house's direction is tested after the realignment. It is refused when the human house is then its only opponent, so the alliance stays one-sided.
- If the named house is the human house, the realignment turns the trigger's computer house hostile again at once, and the alliance is broken in both directions.
